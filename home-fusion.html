Work / Home Fusion
Case study — 04

Turning a Frankfurt trade-fair stand into buyer commitment.

Home Fusion is a Pakistan-based B2B home-textile export business — cushions, throws, tea towels, bedding and totes, sold to buyers rather than direct consumers. Over two years and two Heimtextil fairs in Frankfurt, I worked the commercial and creative side of the stand: product development and selection, stand direction, buyer engagement, and turning a conversation at the booth into an actual order.

RoleCommercial & Creative
Duration2 years, two Heimtextil fairs
ScopeProduct development · Exhibition strategy · Stand direction · Buyer engagement · Sourcing liaison
01

Situation

Home Fusion needed someone at Heimtextil — one of the textile industry's largest trade fairs, held annually in Frankfurt — who could do more than staff the stand: read which products a European buyer would actually commit to, and carry a conversation from interested to ordered, inside a few days on the show floor.

02

Decision

I took on the stand itself, not just the products on it — exhibition strategy and stand direction, deciding what got displayed and how — alongside acting as a sourcing liaison between visiting European buyers and the company, helping match what buyers were actually asking for against what could be produced.

The Home Fusion stand at Heimtextil — the room I was directly responsible for.
03

Approach

Day to day at the fair meant product development and selection across the range — tufted and fringed cushion covers, totes, tea towels, printed bedding — direction of how the stand itself presented that range, and direct buyer engagement: sitting down with visiting buyers, walking them through the collection, and handling customer care once a relationship started.

04

Testing

Fabric and pattern choices were tested directly against buyer reaction on the floor — which colorways and textures drew people in versus which needed more explanation — and against the compliance credentials international buyers actually asked about: OEKO-TEX Standard 100, BSCI social-compliance membership, and ISO 9001 registration.

The compliance credentials that came up in nearly every serious buyer conversation.
05

Signal

Repeat interest across both fairs was the clearest signal of what was working — product lines that consistently pulled buyers into a real sit-down conversation, like the tufted cushion range and the printed bedding, became the ones worth carrying forward to the next fair.

The printed bedding line — one of the ranges that consistently drew buyers in.
06

Action

Once a buyer showed real interest, the job shifted to closing: sitting with them at the stand, going through specifics, and, where needed, acting as the sourcing link between what a buyer wanted and what the company could actually produce and ship.

07

Result

Two Heimtextil fairs in, that's meant real buyer meetings converted at the stand — not just booth traffic, but people sitting down, going through the range, and moving toward an order.

On the show floor, Heimtextil, Frankfurt.
08

Learning

A trade-fair stand is a different kind of conversion moment than a Meta ad or a campaign post — there's no retargeting, no second chance if the read on a buyer is wrong in the room. It sharpened a specific skill: making the sourcing and commercial case for a product, face to face, in the time it takes someone to stop walking.
