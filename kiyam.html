Work / KIYAM
Case study — 02

Taking KIYAM's creative direction from concept to a finished campaign.

KIYAM Dhedhi is a Pakistani bridal and luxury prêt label. I've been a partner and creative director there for two years — responsible for the brand's visual identity, its shoots and campaigns, and, increasingly, the production and business side behind them: sourcing pieces from designers, managing exhibitions and pop-ups, and building the brand's reach toward an international clientele.

RolePartner & Creative Director
Duration2 years, ongoing
ScopeBrand identity · Creative direction · Production · Exhibitions · Clientele
01

Situation

KIYAM needed someone who could take its visual identity and its collections from a concept to something people actually see, recognize and buy into — not one strong campaign, but a consistent presence, collection after collection, backed by production that could actually deliver what the campaign promised.

02

Decision

I came in as a partner, not an outside consultant — which meant the production problems became mine to solve alongside the creative ones, rather than a brief I could hand off once a concept was approved.

KIYAM campaign photography — the finished end of the work I'm responsible for from concept onward.
03

Approach

Creative direction across brand identity and named collections — including Nureh Pret '25 and the premium tier launched as SOLENNE, "the silence of luxury." That means art direction and styling on set, but also sourcing and getting pieces actually made — working directly with the designers and artisans doing the embellishment and embroidery by hand.

04

Testing

Testing ran through the collections themselves: person-named prêt pieces — Laleh, Rosa, Liya, Zahra, Myra among them — sat alongside a distinct premium line, SOLENNE, to read which register the audience responded to. Content mixed polished campaign stills with unfiltered behind-the-scenes video to see what actually built the following, rather than assuming the finished shot was always the stronger post.

SOLENNE — the premium line, launched as its own moment within the brand.
05

Signal

Individual posts have reached well beyond the account's typical range — several into the tens of thousands of views, one past 100K — a clear signal that both the campaign photography and the behind-the-scenes content were landing with people outside KIYAM's existing following.

06

Action

The behind-the-scenes side of that content isn't staged. Coordinating embroiderers and production runs against a hard collection deadline is real, and it's been posted as it happened — stress included — rather than only the finished result. Alongside the creative work, that's extended into managing KIYAM's exhibitions and pop-ups directly, and building toward an international clientele — moving the brand's pieces beyond the domestic market.

07

Result

Two years in, KIYAM has a recognizable visual identity carried consistently across named collections, a premium sub-brand — SOLENNE — that's launched and live, and organic content performance strong enough to regularly outperform typical reach for an account its size.

Campaign content, styled and directed on set.
08

Learning

The gap between "the concept is approved" and "the collection actually ships" is where most of the real decisions happen — fabric that isn't in yet, an artisan running behind, a shoot day that isn't ready when the light is. Being a partner rather than an outside creative meant I couldn't hand that gap to someone else; closing it became part of the job.
